# Session 05 research brief: Keeping customers (layer 4, Relationship)

For the builder. Everything below was opened and checked on 2026-10-16. Figures are in `data/sources-05.json`, examples in `data/examples-05.json`, image credits in `data/credits-05.json`. Images live in `assets/img/s05/`. Nothing here needs a search; if something is missing it is listed under "Mock up instead" or "Could not verify".

Big idea to keep in front of you: the cheapest new job is from a customer you already served. Plumbing has built-in repeat work (tank cleaning, pump servicing, pre-monsoon checks, annual maintenance contracts), and most firms never ask for it.

House rules that bite here: no em or en dashes; none of the words on the rule 1 banned list (say "repeat customers", "enquiries", "times seen"); every number carries publisher, title, year, URL under it; global figures are labelled global. One real example is a Pune firm whose trading name is the word "Synergy" (Synergy Repairs and Maintenance); that is a company name, not jargon, and it is optional, so drop it if the reviewer objects.

### 2. Guess the number (minutes 0 to 3)

Poll: "How much more does it cost to win a new customer than to keep one you already have?" Options: about the same / 2 times / 5 to 25 times / 50 times.

Answer on click: **5 to 25 times**.
Source line under the number: Harvard Business Review, "The Value of Keeping the Right Customers" (Amy Gallo), 2014, hbr.org/2014/10/the-value-of-keeping-the-right-customers.
Exact text on the page: "Depending on which study you believe, and what industry you're in, acquiring a new customer is anywhere from five to 25 times more expensive than retaining an existing one."
Must say on the slide: the ratio varies by industry (the brief requires it, and the source says it). Global figure, no Indian equivalent found; say "global".
Second reveal on the same slide or the next: **keeping 5% more customers lifts profits by 25% to 95%** (same HBR page, citing Frederick Reichheld of Bain and Company; Bain's own 2006 page says "as much as 95 per cent"). Source id `s05-hbr-bain-retention-profit`.
Screenshot of the source: `hbr-value-of-keeping-customers.png` (crop off the blank ad band at the top).

### 6. Reminders that bring work back (minutes 8 to 11)

12-month calendar graphic. Anchor the seasons on IMD:
- Monsoon normally reaches Kerala around **1 June** and has left the whole country around **15 October** (India Meteorological Department, Monsoon Information: Onset page and Monsoon FAQ PDF). Source id `s05-imd-monsoon-onset-withdrawal`. Screenshot `imd-monsoon-onset.png`.
- Suggested calendar (illustrative, label it): April and May, pre-monsoon checks (roof drains, overflow pipes, sump pumps). June to September, monsoon, emergency cover and drainage. October and November, post-monsoon tank cleaning and pump servicing. December to March, annual maintenance contract renewals and bathroom refits.
Images: `pexels-dibakar-roy-monsoon-kolkata.jpg` (monsoon street, Kolkata) and `pexels-wafiq-raza-rooftop-blue-water-tank.jpg` (rooftop tanks). Both Pexels, credited.
Real examples that sell the repeat visit: Kirloskar Brothers e-shop Services page (`kirloskar-pumps-amc-services.png`, two services only: Installation/Inspection and Annual Maintenance Contract) and Residenso Solutions, Pune (`residenso-pune-society-maintenance.png`, society maintenance menu with Plumbing, Tank Cleaning, Drainage Cleaning). Optional one-liner from Synergy Repairs and Maintenance, Pune: "Annual maintenance contracts that keep plumbing, electrical and civil upkeep on a fixed yearly schedule, instead of a call only when something breaks." (synergyrm.in, quote it rather than show the cartoon page.)
WhatsApp reminder, mockup in WhatsApp style with an "illustrative" label. Draft text: "Namaste Mrs Kulkarni, Sharma Plumbing Works here. Monsoon is four weeks away. We cleaned your society's overhead tanks last October. Would you like us to check the roof drains and sump pump before the rains? Reply YES and we will call to fix a time. Reply STOP to opt out."

### 7. Broadcast lists done right (minutes 11 to 13)

Side by side, both mockups in WhatsApp style, labelled illustrative.
Welcome: short, named, one useful thing, one clear next step, opt-out line, sent in office hours, from a saved contact.
Blocked: a forwarded festival poster, no name, sent to everyone, "Dear customer", a discount for a service they never bought, 9 pm on a Sunday.
Facts for the side bar, from WhatsApp's own help page (`whatsapp-faq-broadcast-lists.png`, faq.whatsapp.com/861663048350950): a broadcast list holds **up to 256 contacts**, and **only people who have saved your number receive it**. Source id `s05-whatsapp-broadcast-limit`. That second rule is the lesson: the list is only as good as the people who want to hear from you.

### 8. Referrals and reviews: the moment to ask (minutes 13 to 15)

The moment: the day the job is signed off and the customer says thank you, not the invoice day.
Review request, mockup in WhatsApp style: "Thank you for choosing Sharma Plumbing Works, Mr Deshpande. If the work went well, a two-line Google review helps other societies in Kothrud find us: [link]. If anything is not right, reply here and Aditya will come back within 48 hours."
Numbers (all BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, label "global survey, US and UK"): **83%** of people asked to leave a review went on to leave one (`s05-brightlocal-asked-left-review`); **97%** read reviews for local businesses (`s05-brightlocal-read-reviews`); **80%** are likely to use a business that replies to all its reviews (`s05-brightlocal-respond-all-reviews`). Screenshot `brightlocal-review-survey-2026.png`. Use one or two, not all three, on one slide.
Official rules: Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to get more reviews" (`google-help-tips-more-reviews.png`): ask with a Google link or QR code, reply to reviews, never offer incentives for reviews (prohibited). "Manage customer reviews" (`google-help-manage-reviews.png`): a verified business can reply and the reviewer is notified.

### 11. Sharma Plumbing Works: before and after (minutes 15 to 19 in the running order)

Both mockups in WhatsApp style, labelled illustrative.
Before: "Payment received, thank you." Nothing else, ever.
After: three messages over six months. Day 0: thank you, name, review link, "reply if anything is not right". Month 5: reminder that tank cleaning is due before monsoon, reply YES to book. Month 6 on: the seasonal calendar message.
Poll: "Which one would bring you back?" Options: Sharma before / Sharma after. Reveal nothing; it answers itself.

### 12. Who does what (minutes 19 to 21)

Owner decides what the business promises after every job (the three messages and the seasons). Team sends and logs in the sheet. Agency sets up WhatsApp Business, labels, the sheet template and the review link. Three questions to ask: how many repeat jobs last quarter; who owns the customer list and where is it; what did customers hear from us after the job.

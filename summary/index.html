IPA Digital Knowledge Series

Six sessions for board members, chapter heads and business owners

The series is one 30-minute online session a month, presented over Zoom by Dhruv Tewari, founder of The Strategy Lab, for the IPA fraternity. It is written for senior people who direct the work rather than do it: contractors, consultants, manufacturers and dealers, and the chapter heads who look after members. Every session has the same shape. One real market fact, one map of how digital fits together, one topic explained in plain English, one fictional plumbing firm shown before and after, a clear view of who should do what, and one thing to check this week. The decks stay on this site afterwards so that any of IPA's 7,500 plus members across 28 chapters can open them without a presenter.

Why now: 886 million Indians were active internet users in 2024, and more of them live in rural India (488 million) than in cities (397 million). The customer who used to ask a neighbour for a plumber now asks a phone first. Most plumbing firms are excellent at the work and almost invisible where that search happens. The series closes that gap in a way senior people can direct, judge and measure.

IAMAI and Kantar, Internet in India 2024 (ICUBE 2024), 2024. Read the source

The six sessions

Session 1

What "digital" actually means

Digital is not "being on Instagram". It is five layers that hand a customer from one to the next, and most businesses only have one or two switched on.

Guess the number

886 million

Indians were active internet users in 2024, and more of them live in rural India (488 million) than in cities (397 million).

IAMAI and Kantar, Internet in India 2024 (ICUBE 2024), 2024. Read the source

Three ideas covered

  • The five-layer map, with the platforms that sit in each layer.
  • One buyer's journey: an architect in Pune hiring a plumbing contractor for a hospital extension, from Google search to the annual maintenance contract, each step lighting a layer.
  • Where businesses leak: usually Home (no or stale Google profile) and Relationship (no follow-up after the job).

Sharma Plumbing Works

Before, a bare Google listing with no photos next to a well-run contractor's listing; after, the same search with Sharma looking like the firm it actually is.

Who does what

  • Agency runs the layers that need specialist hands.

Session 2

Your home online

Before you spend a rupee on ads, fix where people land. For most plumbing businesses that is the Google profile, not the website.

Guess the number

76 out of 100

People who search on their phone for something nearby visit a business within a day.

Google (Think with Google), How Mobile Search Connects Consumers to Stores, 2016. Read the source

Three ideas covered

  • Google Business Profile as the standard: category, service areas, hours, photos, reviews and posts, with a checklist to tick against your own.
  • The website decision: sell products online (Shopify), a site you update yourself (WordPress), or portals and member logins (custom build, as IPA's own site is). Five seconds on the home page: what you do, where, proof, how to contact.
  • WhatsApp Business done properly: business profile, catalogue, quick replies, away message.

Sharma Plumbing Works

Before, a Google profile with no photos, wrong hours and two old reviews; after, 30 real-work photos, service areas, 40 reviews and replies to each.

Who does what

  • Owner approves what the business claims to be and where it works.
  • Team keeps photos and reviews current.
  • Agency builds the website if one is needed.

For a business: open your Google profile today and check who holds the login.

For a chapter: does the chapter have a findable page with events and a contact?

Session 3

Being found and trusted

Each platform has one job. Pick the one or two where your buyers already are.

Guess the number

What share of business buyers research a vendor and its people online before they ever get in touch? The figure and its source are confirmed in the deck once the research for this session is complete.

Three ideas covered

  • One platform, one job: LinkedIn for credibility with architects, consultants and developers; Instagram for finished work and the team; YouTube for walkthroughs that keep being found; Pinterest for fittings and bathroom brands only.
  • What to post when you have no time: before and after, a site walkthrough, one tip a week, a client thank-you. Phone video is enough.
  • Reviews and reputation: the three-line reply to a one-star review, and why the owner's own LinkedIn often does more than the company page.

Sharma Plumbing Works

Before, an empty LinkedIn for the owner and a company Instagram with three posts; after, both showing real projects, real people and a steady rhythm.

Who does what

  • Owner picks two platforms, appears in content now and then, approves the tone.
  • Team shoots on site.
  • Agency plans, edits and posts.

For a business: look at your last five posts as a client would.

For a chapter: share one photo and three lines from the last meeting, tagging IPA.

Session 4

Paying for reach

Ads multiply what exists. They do not fix a weak home.

Guess the number

59%

Digital took 59% of all advertising money spent in India in 2025, Rs 71,621 crore. Television took 21% and print 14%.

dentsu India and exchange4media, dentsu-e4m Digital Advertising Report 2026, 2026. Read the source

Three ideas covered

  • Three platforms, three jobs: Google ads catch people already searching; Meta ads reach people in your city who are not searching yet; LinkedIn ads reach decision-makers by title and company.
  • How a small test works: four weeks, one platform, one city, one offer, a fixed budget, then decide with numbers. Cost per enquiry is simply spend divided by enquiries.
  • Mistakes that waste money: boosting random posts aimed at all of India, a vague ad, no way to know where the call came from. For chapters, promoting an event such as the Indian Plumbing Conference with a small budget and a registration link.

Sharma Plumbing Works

Before, Rs 5,000 on a boosted post aimed at all of India; after, the same Rs 5,000 on a Google ad for one service in Pune.

Who does what

  • Owner sets the goal, the budget and the one offer.
  • Team answers every enquiry within the hour.
  • Agency runs the ads.

For a business: write down one offer, one city and one monthly budget. Do not spend yet.

For a chapter: pick the next event that could use a small push.

Session 5

Keeping customers

The cheapest new job is from a customer you already served. Plumbing has built-in repeat work, and most firms never ask for it.

Guess the number

5 to 25 times

Winning a new customer costs five to 25 times more than keeping an existing one, depending on the industry. This is a global figure; no Indian equivalent was found.

Harvard Business Review, The Value of Keeping the Right Customers (Amy Gallo), 2014. Read the source

Three ideas covered

  • The customer list as an asset: name, number, job, date, next service due, in one place.
  • Reminders that bring work back: pre-monsoon checks, tank cleaning, pump servicing, annual maintenance contracts, sent as a WhatsApp message people welcome rather than block.
  • Referrals and reviews: the moment to ask, and what one extra repeat job a year per customer is worth. For chapters, a short monthly update that keeps members engaged between events.

Sharma Plumbing Works

Before, the message after a job reads "Payment received, thank you"; after, it says thank you, offers a review link and books a reminder in six months.

Who does what

  • Owner decides what the business promises after the job.
  • Team sends the messages and logs them.
  • Agency sets up the tools.

For a business: list your last 20 customers and send one useful seasonal reminder.

For a chapter: one monthly update with a member spotlight.

Session 6

Measuring it, plus the new tools

You do not need a dashboard. You need five numbers once a month.

Guess the number

31%

Only 31% of people surveyed in India had tried any generative AI tool, and 60% were not familiar with AI at all.

Google India with Kantar, 75% of Indians desire a daily growth collaborator: Google-Kantar report (Google India Blog), 2025. Read the source

Three ideas covered

  • The one-page scorecard: profile views, enquiries, jobs won, reviews, repeat customers, and where each number lives on the platforms' own screens.
  • Reading the numbers: views up but enquiries flat; enquiries up but jobs flat; jobs up but no reviews. The likely cause and the question to ask.
  • AI tools that save time: drafting a post or a review reply, writing a quote, turning a phone video into a captioned reel, and the one check before anything goes out. Closes with the 90-day plan: all six "one thing to check" items on one page.

Sharma Plumbing Works

Before, the agency's monthly report is 14 pages of reach and likes; after, it is one page with five numbers and one decision.

Who does what

  • Owner reads five numbers monthly and decides.
  • Team or agency produces the numbers with their sources.
  • Agency explains what changed and why.

For a business: fill the one-page scorecard for last month.

For a chapter: report the chapter's five numbers to IPA HQ monthly.
